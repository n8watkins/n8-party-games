How a night works

Five things happen, over and over.

  1. 1

    Join

    Whoever starts the party gets a four-digit code and a QR code on the room's wall. Friends scan it or type the code, pick a name, and they are in. Up to four play.

  2. 2

    Walk

    You are a small piece in a side-view room. Walk, jump, and knock your friends about. On a phone the controls are buttons under your thumbs; on a laptop they are the arrow keys.

  3. 3

    Vote

    Every game is a box on the wall with a lamp over it. Standing in its pool of light is your vote. When everyone is in a light, a three-second count starts. Step out and it stops.

    A tie is a coin toss. Everybody watches it. And yes, you can bump a friend out of a light before the count ends.

  4. 4

    Play

    The game with the most pieces in its light wins the vote and the whole party plays it, each on their own device.

    Where this is today: Cork & Bell plays right inside the room, a stall each, for ninety seconds. Who Am I? still sends its two players to its own site.

  5. 5

    Score

    Everyone lands back in the room. The Tonight chalkboard gives 3 points for first, 2 for second and 1 for third, and keeps the tally across every game you play that night.

    Tonight

    1. Dolly7
    2. Mabel5
    3. Tom4
    4. Fitch2

    after 3 games

Then the lights come back on, and you vote again.

The pieces

Pick who you are. Each one has a move.

One move per piece, on a cooldown, for getting in the way of a vote. Today everyone is a pawn, and the pawn's knock-back works. The other three pieces and their moves are designed and drawn, not playable yet.

  • Playable today

    Pawn

    Charge. Wind up, slam, and whoever it lands on is knocked back out of their light. It works today, with a cooldown before the next one.

  • Coming

    Robot

    Hook. A claw on a chain, fired from the chest. It drags one player across the floor to the robot.

  • Coming

    Slime

    Absorb or harden. Hold to go soft and turn a hook around. Tap to go hard and throw a charge back. Guess wrong and nothing happens.

  • Coming

    Ghost

    Fade. For a moment nothing can touch it. The catch: a faded ghost does not count as a vote.

The rooms

Same room, different night.

A room is a skin. The lamps, the boxes and the rules stay where they are, so if you know one room you know them all. Today the room is the bar. The other five are concept drawings, and they are being built.

Big screen and phones

One room on every screen.

  • The big screen

    Open the site on any laptop or TV, type the code, and choose "Use this as the room's screen". It shows the whole room for everyone and nobody plays from it.

  • Your phone

    Hold it sideways. You see the same room, live, closer in on your own piece, with the buttons under your thumbs. One tap pulls back to the whole room.

  • No big screen? Fine.

    Phones alone can run the room. A game that needs a shared screen keeps its light off and says so.

The two screens above are concept drawings of the room's interface. Today's room is a first playable version and looks plainer.

Open source

Build a game for the room.

The room, the party kit under it, and every game are MIT licensed. Each game is its own repository and plays on its own. A written game contract says what a game must export for the room to host it, so you can make one too.

  • The kit handles codes, seats, reconnects, and bots that take over a dropped seat
  • A game exports two things: its rules for the server, and a manifest
  • Solo play costs nothing to host, and rooms sleep when nobody moves
